Chore Slayer — Privacy Policy

Effective: 2026-06-06 · Operator/contact details in [brackets] must be filled in before publishing.

Chore Slayer is a household chore-and-activity tracker used by a parent or caregiver to manage tasks for the children in their family. This policy explains what the app stores and how it is handled. The app is operated by [YOUR NAME / ORGANIZATION] ("we") and your data is stored on our own private server at chores.a-t-365.com.

What we store

  • Children's first and last names, plus an optional icon/color, entered by the parent.
  • Chores and activities and their schedules.
  • Daily check-offs (which tasks were completed each day).
  • Daily reports a parent may write: mood, a 1–5 behavior rating, highlights, challenges, notes, and goals.
  • A parent PIN, stored only as a secure one-way hash.

We do not collect device identifiers, location, contacts, or advertising data, and the app contains no third-party advertising or analytics SDKs.

How we use it

The information is used solely to provide the app's features for your household — showing each child their tasks, tracking completion, and producing the parent and BHIS reports. We do not use it for advertising or profiling.

How it's shared

We do not sell your data and do not share it with third parties. Data stays on the operator's server. (If you self-host, you are the operator and controller of that data.)

Children's privacy

Chore Slayer is a tool for parents and caregivers. A parent enters and controls all children's information behind a PIN; children only tap to check off their own tasks. We collect the minimum needed (a name) and never use children's data for advertising or tracking.

Retention & deletion

Parents control the data at any time: removing a child hides them but keeps their reports for your records, and "Delete forever" permanently erases that child and all of their tasks, check-offs, and reports. To delete all data, contact us or remove it directly.

Security

All traffic uses HTTPS. The parent PIN is stored as a secure hash, repeated wrong-PIN attempts are rate-limited, and parent actions require a signed-in session.
